03. My Strategy for Addressing the Challenges

After the pilot, users found the app's UI and certain features unintuitive. I led the next design steps by brainstorming with the Product Owner and Tech team to address these challenges.

Key Decisions
  • Committed to a full redesign, not patch fixes: color, hierarchy, and signifiers were inconsistent enough that surface-level changes wouldn't fix the "immature" feeling users reported.
  • Rebuilt around visual hierarchy and a calmer palette: replaced the cluttered, text-heavy layout with clearer IA and a warm, muted cream palette so the app itself felt less stressful to use.
  • Owned QA personally to kill the crashes: stress-tested and load-tested the app myself ahead of re-pilot, since reliability is non-negotiable for a tool people reach for under stress.
  • Shipped notifications as a new retention lever: introduced as a net-new feature (not a V1 carryover) specifically to bring users back, and got it working reliably this time.
  • Validated with a focus group before shipping: tested the redesign with a small group prior to re-pilot launch.
My Approach

I analyzed the information architecture, identified friction points, and proposed alternatives. Users found the UI immature due to inconsistencies in color, text hierarchy, and signifiers, leading us to a full redesign. This time, I focused on these core foundational principles:

User Centricity

With a clear understanding of user preferences, I improved existing features, fixed pain points, and ensured a user-centric redesign through focus group testing.

Seamless and Stunning

I aimed for more than usability. I wanted seamless interactions where every transition felt meaningful, creating a wow-worthy experience. To ensure this, I tested it with a small focus group.

05. Results After the Redesign
Retention

Retention climbed from the ~2% industry baseline for health & wellness apps to 17.4% after the redesign, driven by cleaner information architecture and a calmer visual language.

Completion

Meaningfully more users completed the full stress-check journey end-to-end, from flagging they were stressed to reaching a resolution, once the IA overhaul removed friction from the flow.

Reliability

I led stress- and load-testing ahead of re-pilot, and the crashes reported in V1 were no longer an issue, which is non-negotiable for a tool people reach for under stress.

What InKind Told Us

Per InKind's pilot feedback, staff reported higher tips, more consistent clock-ins, and lower turnover, while managers gained visibility into team stress they could act on.

*Self-reported via pilot feedback, not measured analytics
