01

Why V4

V2 worked. The question was what people would do with an app that had earned ninety seconds of their day, and what the wellness space had become while we were measuring.

What V2 proved

People stayed for the resets and the calm.

Retention went from the ~2% industry baseline to 17.4%, daily engagement from 12% to 56%, and the average session ran 4m 20s. Quick resets and a quiet UI were the whole reason.

What users asked for

Ask for less, earlier. Give more, later.

Onboarding asked for trust before the app had done anything. Coping points piled up with no clear value. And once the workday ended there was nothing to do in the app at all.

Where the space went

Wellness left the app and went into the room.

Run clubs, sound baths, dinner nights, Luma and Partiful style events. The product that wins is the one that gets you there, not the one with the best breathing timer.

The bet: Community. Make the check-in pay for something real, off work, with other people.

02

Check in, then act

Slide to answer. The stress shape names the feeling instead of grading it, the release opens a panel of things to do, and finishing one lands a coin.

The shape. Seven levels, orange through green to violet. Never red.

The panel. Two films, two quick resets, a way to talk.

The reset. Forty-five seconds. Pause, or say you're done.

The coin. One beat for the coin, one for the words.

The shape is the one thing on the screen that changes with you. It sharpens and cools as stress climbs, so a bad day looks different without ever looking like an error. The words stay about the feeling: running hot, not stress level 7 of 10.

DecisionA check-in that leads nowhere wastes the ninety seconds. Every check-in ends in something to do, and every reset ends in coins that are good for something.
03

Off Work: the map

Flip Home from At Work to Off Work and it becomes a map of wellness experiences nearby. The coins from the workday spend here.

The map. Partners and open places, told apart by pin. Chips for spas, parks, movement, open now.

The sheet. Wellness experiences and community events, one list, three stops.

The place. Reviews, amenities, and one labelled action: redeem for 80 coins.

The code. A QR and a code for the front desk, valid until the date it says.

Every place carries a real offer with a real price in coins. Too few coins reads top up, never a dead button. A redeemed place keeps its code on the card so the front desk is a single tap away, and the map shows what's open right now before anything else.

DecisionTrust arrives late, on purpose. Location is asked for the first time the map is useful, not on day one, and the first check-in happens before there is an account at all.

Community: the flagship

Events on the same map. Breathwork circles, pottery socials, dinner nights, a quiet hour online. Reserve one with coins, or host your own.

The list. Hosted by Stressie, partners and people.

The event. When, where, how far, who's going.

Reserve. A check, then confetti.

You're going. Calendar, share, done.

Host. Name, about, when, spots, price.

Preview. Exactly the page guests will see.

Sent for review. In person waits; online lists now.

Reserving costs coins, and the coins came from check-ins, so a seat has a cost the person already paid in attention. Hosting is one form: a name, a line about it, when, capacity, in person or online, free or a price in coins. Thirteen Figma frames, thirteen build states, matched one to one.

DecisionOnline events list instantly. In-person ones wait for approval, because a stranger's address needs a person to look at it first.
DecisionReserving costs coins. A free seat gets skipped; a seat paid for with a week of check-ins gets shown up to.
DecisionThe cover is suggested from the name and the description, so a host never faces an empty photo well or publishes a grey card.
05

Coins, Talk and Insights

Three more pieces, one screen each. The economy that pays for the map, a voice for the days a reset isn't enough, and the view a team lead gets.

Reset coins

A real economy, not points.

Five coins a check-in, up to three a day. A reset once a day, past halfway. Two hundred when a friend checks in. Short of a reward, top up. Coins leave only through a labelled action, once.

Talk

An orb that listens.

Resting, listening, thinking and speaking each have a colour; the light stays warm whatever the last check-in said. Anything that reads as crisis gets a handoff to 988, not a chat. It says so, and stops.

Insights, B2B

Your team is steadier than it was.

Stress across the team over time, the top stressors, and huddles to run. Managers get a fifth tab; nobody else sees it, and no individual is ever shown.

Craft: the rules that held

The design system lives in code. Screens never name a colour, a radius or a type size, and a lint fails the build when one does.

Amber is for action
One amber element per screen, the thing you want tapped. A second one makes both weaker.
Sage is for reassurance
Breathing, chat, you're fine. It is never an action colour.
Never red
Stress is not an error state. High stress runs amber through violet on the shape's own scale. Red only ends something.
Lora and Hanken
Serif for the one thing the screen is about. Sans for everything you read rather than look at. Sentence case everywhere.
Liquid Glass, real glass
glassEffect on iOS 26 with material fallbacks. Nav carries no tint of ours; content passes under the floating tab bar.
Dynamic Type, and a lint
Every text is a role that scales with the reader. A build phase fails any screen that names a raw colour or size.
Event page, Figma render beside the buildMeasured at 3 px/pt, one anchor per pane
