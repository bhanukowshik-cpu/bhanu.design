Turning a ninety-second check-in into plans after work.

Stressie is a workplace wellness app. V2 got people to check in and use quick resets. In V4 I designed and built what comes next: coins earned by checking in, a map of wellness experiences to spend them on, and community events people can reserve or host.

Three bets

I made three bets. The rest of the page shows how they played out.

Bet 1

Every check-in leads to an action within the same ninety seconds.

Bet 2

Coins buy real things outside the app, or they are just points.

Bet 3

Community is the product. The app gets you there.

Bet 1 · At Work

Check in, then act, then earn.

You slide to answer. The stress shape shows the level instead of a number. When you release the slider the check-in is saved and a panel opens with two short films, two quick resets and a way to talk. Finishing a reset earns coins.

The shape has seven levels, orange to violet. Never red.

The panel opens on release, not on a button.

The reset is 45 seconds. You can pause or stop.

The coin lands first, then the words.

The shape is the only element that changes with your answer. It gets sharper and cooler as stress rises, so a bad day looks different without looking like an error. The copy names the feeling, running hot, instead of grading it.

What building it changedI wired the release gesture as the commit, so there is no Continue button. The panel is driven by the same drag, and the coin fires from the reset's completion handler, so a reward cannot appear before the work is done.

Bet 2 · Off Work

Switch to Off Work and Home becomes a map.

The same Home screen has an Off Work mode: a map of wellness experiences nearby. Partner venues and public places get different pins, and chips filter for spas, parks, movement and open now. Every place shows a real offer with a price in coins.

The map, with pins, chips and a three-stop sheet.

The list: wellness experiences and community events.

A place page: reviews, amenities and one action, redeem for 80 coins.

The code and QR you show at the front desk.

What building it changedI ask for location the first time the map is opened, not during onboarding, and the first check-in happens before there is an account. If you are short of coins the button reads "Top up" instead of going dead.

Bet 3 · Community, the flagship

Reserve a seat with your coins, or host an event.

Events live on the same map: breathwork circles, pottery socials, dinner nights, a quiet hour online. You reserve with coins, add the event to your calendar and share it. Hosting is one form: name, description, time, capacity, in person or online, free or paid.

The economy

The coin economy: earn, top up, share, spend.

You earn five coins per check-in, up to three a day, and more for finishing a reset. Inviting a friend who checks in earns 200. If you are short, you can top up. Coins are only spent through a labelled button, once.

Earn: five per check-in, more for a finished reset.

Spend: your balance, the next reward and coins to go.

Top up: four amounts, Apple Pay or a card.

Share: the invite flow, the one screen with its own colour.

Designing the coin

One coin, rendered once, used everywhere.

I went through four versions before this one: an opal gem in Metal, a fire agate coin in RealityKit, a wreath coin in four palettes, and a honey centre. Each was rejected for a reason. The final coin is gold with three raised petals, real geometry, rendered from one scene.

Opal gemBeautiful, not a coin
Agate coinRead as lava
WreathTwo objects
Honey centreWarm, lost the shape

The reward moment with four lighting options: peach beam, gold beam, house light, rays. The upward fan won; a burst read as Duolingo.

Before drawing the reward moment I studied how Apple Fitness, Duolingo, Headspace and Robinhood celebrate. The result is two beats: the coin rises and settles on a spring with no bounce in the rotation, then the text lands.

What building it changedThe haptic fires from the coin's completion handler, on the visual landing. With Reduce Motion on, the coin fades in at rest, the progress bar still fills and the haptic still plays.

Design to build

I designed it in Figma and built it in SwiftUI.

The design system lives in code. Screens never name a colour, radius or type size, and a lint fails the build if one does.

What I would do next

  • Put a real reviewer behind the approval queue before the first in-person host signs up.
  • Give Talk a model, and keep the crisis handoff as direct as it is.
  • Working alone was fast. The next version needs a second engineer reviewing the code before the numbers are trusted.
