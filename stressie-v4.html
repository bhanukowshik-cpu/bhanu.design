Three bets

Judge the screens against these.

Bet 1

A check-in has to lead somewhere inside the same ninety seconds.

Asking how someone feels and then doing nothing with it wastes the one moment they gave you.

Bet 2

Coins have to buy something real, off work, or they are points.

A price in the room, a code at the front desk, a seat with your name on it.

Bet 3

Community is the product. The app is how you get there.

The wellness space went in person. Stressie's job is to be the map and the ticket.

Bet 1 · At Work

Check in, then act, then get paid.

Slide to answer. The stress shape names the feeling instead of grading it. Releasing the slider logs the check-in and raises a panel with two films, two quick resets and a way to talk. Finishing a reset lands the coin.

The shape. Seven levels, orange through green to violet. Never red.

The panel. Rises on the release, not on a button.

The reset. Forty-five seconds. Pause, or say you're done.

The coin. One beat for the coin, one for the words.

The shape is the only thing on the screen that changes with you. It sharpens and cools as stress climbs, so a bad day looks different without looking like an error. The words stay about the feeling: running hot, not 7 out of 10.

Because I wrote the codeThe release gesture, not a Continue button, is the commit. The panel is driven off the same drag, and the coin lands on the reset's own completion handler, so the reward can never fire before the work is done.

Bet 2 · Off Work

Flip Home, and the city becomes the app.

The same Home has an Off Work mode: a map of wellness experiences nearby, partners and open places told apart by pin, chips for spas, parks, movement and open now. Every place carries a real offer with a real price in coins.

The map. Pins, chips, and a sheet with three stops.

The list. Wellness experiences and community events, one sheet.

The place. Reviews, amenities, one labelled action: redeem for 80 coins.

The code. A QR and a code for the front desk.

Because I wrote the codeLocation is requested the first time the map is useful, from the Off Work toggle, not in onboarding. The first check-in happens before there is an account at all. Too few coins reads "Top up", never a dead button.

Bet 3 · Community, the flagship

Reserve a seat with your check-ins, or host one.

Events on the same map: breathwork circles, pottery socials, dinner nights, a quiet hour online. Reserve for coins, add to calendar, share. Hosting is one form: a name, a line about it, when, spots, in person or online, free or a price.

The economy

A coin you can earn, top up, share and spend.

Five coins a check-in, up to three a day. A reset once a day, past halfway. Two hundred when a friend checks in. Short of a reward, top up. Coins leave only through a labelled action, once, and the words stay about the feeling, not the balance.

Earn. The first coins arrive before the account does.

Spend. Balance, the next reward, coins to go.

Top up. Four tiles, Apple Pay or a card, one CTA.

Share. The one flow allowed its own colour.

Designing the coin

Five studies before one coin was allowed on screen.

There is one coin, and everything that shows a coin shows that one. Getting there took an opal, an agate, a wreath and a rule.

01 · Opal gem

A pattern wrapped in glass, refracted at the rim. Metal shaders. Beautiful, and not a coin.

02 · Agate coin

Real 3D meshes in RealityKit, gold rim, a fire-opal face. Read as lava on a calm screen.

03 · Wreath

Four leaf palettes around a centre. Closer, still two objects.

04 · Centres

World, plain wash, honey amber, teal glass. Honey won the warmth, lost the shape.

05 · The bloom coin

Gold, three petals raised out of the face, real geometry. Rendered once, used everywhere.

The reward moment, four lights: peach beam, gold beam, house light, rays. The sunrise fan won; a burst read as Duolingo.

The reward moment was researched before it was drawn: Apple Fitness clears the stage and centres one object, Headspace celebrates with a breath, Robinhood dropped its confetti under regulator pressure. So: two beats. The coin rises and settles on a spring with no bounce in the rotation, then the words land.

Because I wrote the codeThe haptic fires from the coin's completion handler, on the visual landing. Under Reduce Motion the coin fades in already at rest, the progress bar still fills, and the haptic still plays.

Design to build

One pair of hands, from the research read to the App Store build.

I designed V4 in Figma and built it in SwiftUI myself. The design system lives in code: screens never name a colour, a radius or a type size, and a lint fails the build when one does.

Event page, Figma render beside the build. Measured at 3 px/pt against one anchor per pane.

Talk, glass and glow. Real Liquid Glass on iOS 26 with material fallbacks.

  • Amber is for action. One per screen. A second makes both weaker.
  • Sage is for reassurance. Never an action colour.
  • Never red. Stress is not an error. Red only ends something.
  • Lora for the one thing the screen is about. Hanken for what you read.
  • Every text is a Dynamic Type role. Screens never name a size.
  • Pull-overs are the only presentation. Grabber, from a screen root, tab bar hidden.

English. The language menu sits above the first question.

Spanish. About 1,150 strings relocalise live, answers kept.

Swahili. Layout built for the longest translation first.

What I built: every screen and flow, the design system and its lint, the check-in, resets, map, community, coins, referral, Talk's voice UI, three languages, Reduce Motion and Dynamic Type across all of it.

What I did not build: the backend, payments, and the model behind Talk. Accounts, events and coins are local stores with the seams marked for the team, and the handoff says so.
