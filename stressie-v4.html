I designed and built Stressie V4: a ninety-second stress check-in that pays for your evening.

Check in at your desk, earn coins, spend them on wellness experiences and community events nearby. Designed in Figma, written in SwiftUI, by me.

The problem

V2 got people to check in. Then it had nothing for them to do.

V2 lifted retention from about 2% to 17.4% and daily engagement from 12% to 56%, on quick resets and a calm screen. The pilots also said trust was asked for too early, the points had no value, and the app went quiet after work. Meanwhile wellness moved into the room: run clubs, sound baths, dinners on Luma and Partiful.

"I haven't built trust with the app yet, so sharing personal feelings this early is uncomfortable."Pilot participant, Amazon hardware team
"I didn't find the coping points compelling as an incentive. I don't know their value."Pilot participant, InKind

At Work

The check-in ends in an action, not a score.

You slide to answer and the shape changes with you. Releasing the slider saves the check-in and opens a panel with two short films, two quick resets and a way to talk. Finishing a reset earns coins on the spot.

Seven levels, orange to violet. Never red.

The panel opens on release.

A one-minute reset. Pause or stop.

The coin lands, then the words.

DecisionThe release gesture is the commit. There is no Continue button, so the panel can open on the same drag.
CutThe 0 to 10 score. The shape names the feeling, running hot, and never grades it.

Off Work

Switch to Off Work and Home becomes a map, and only then does it ask where you are.

Partner venues and public places get different pins. Chips filter for open now, spas, parks and movement. Every place shows a real offer with a price in coins, and a redeemed offer becomes a code for the front desk.

Location is asked here, not in onboarding.

The map, with the sheet below it.

A place, its offer, one button.

The code you show at the desk.

DecisionThe first check-in happens before there is an account, and location is requested by the map, not the onboarding.
CutPermission screens on day one. V2 asked for everything up front and the pilots told us it cost trust.

Community

Reserve a seat with your coins, or host an event with one form.

Events live on the same map: breathwork circles, pottery socials, dinner nights, a quiet hour online. Reserving costs coins. Hosting is name, description, time, capacity, in person or online, free or paid, then a preview of exactly what guests will see.

Coins

A coin you can earn, top up, share and spend, and one coin everywhere.

Five coins per check-in, up to three a day, more for a finished reset, 200 for a friend who checks in. Short of a reward, you top up. Coins leave only through a labelled button. The coin itself took four rejected rounds (an opal gem, an agate coin, a wreath, a honey centre) before this one: gold, three raised petals, rendered once from one scene.

Earn. The first coins arrive before the account.

Spend. Balance, next reward, coins to go.

DecisionCoins are only spent through a button that names the price. "Redeem for 80 coins", never "Continue".
CutStreaks and confetti. The reward is two beats: the coin settles, then the words. Nothing shouts.

Designed and built

The same screen in Figma and in SwiftUI. Building it changed the design twice.

The event page. Figma render and simulator build, measured against each other at 3 px/pt.

What building it changed, 1The reward haptic fires from the coin's completion handler, on the visual landing. In Figma the haptic was a note; in code it became the timing.
What building it changed, 2The design system lives in code and a lint fails the build if a screen names a colour, radius or type size. That rule only exists because I was writing both.

What I built: every screen and flow, the design system and its lint, check-in, resets, the map, community, coins, referral, Talk's voice UI, three languages, Reduce Motion and Dynamic Type. What I did not build: the backend, payments and the model behind Talk. Those run on local stores with the seams marked for the team.

The call I owned

In-person events wait for approval. It costs every host a day.

The founders wanted every event live instantly. I held the line on in-person events because a stranger's address needs a human check before Stressie puts its name beside it. The cost is real: a host waits, and some will not come back. Whether I was right is one of the things being measured now.

What I would do differently

  • Put a real reviewer behind the approval queue before the first host signs up. Right now the queue is a state, not a person.
  • I shipped the coin before the economy had a sink. Redemptions should have been live on day one.
  • Working alone was fast. The next version needs a second engineer reading the code before the numbers are trusted.
