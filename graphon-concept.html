How I'd show an AI's memory.

Graphon says its AI “remembers what it has read, heard, seen and understands how it all connects.” That connection is the product, so I think people should be able to see it.

Here is one screen for that: ask a question, get an answer where every sentence points to its source, and watch the video, PDF, audio and spreadsheet it connected light up as a graph. Try it below.

    Four design calls behind this screen.

    The company, files and questions in the demo are made up. The design calls are the part I'd bring to the job.

    1. Every sentence carries its source.

      A citation opens the exact place: the page of the PDF, the second of the video, the moment in the call, the row of the sheet. People trust an answer they can check in one click.

    2. Show the path, not just the pile.

      Graphon's site says retrieval starts from a few matches and follows connections to what the answer needs. The graph replays that in order: what matched first, then what it followed.

    3. Say plainly what it couldn't find.

      A gap gets its own line, as clear as the answer. An enterprise user acting on a wrong date is worse than one who knows the date is missing.

    4. One colour means “used in this answer”.

      Everything the answer didn't use stays quiet, so a graph of thousands of files still reads in a glance.

    What I'd want to learn first.

    • Who reads the answer: a person at a desk, or an agent calling the Answer API? The screen changes a lot between the two.
    • How big real graphs get: at a few thousand linked sources, the graph should zoom to the answer's neighbourhood, not show everything.
    • What people do when a citation is wrong: that moment needs a way to say so, and it is the best signal for improving answers.
